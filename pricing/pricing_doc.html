1. Executive summary

This document redesigns the Car QR Showcase pricing model around a product you do not sell yet: a premium, full-colour printed sticker that you produce and ship, rather than a PNG the dealer prints on their own office printer. The cost of producing that sticker is real, and it changes the shape of the business.

The five findings that shape this strategy
  1. There is no payment integration in the product. The plan and price fields are labels on a card — nothing charges anyone. A monthly subscription is hard to collect in Iraq; a prepaid physical product is not.
  2. Your cost base swings 6× on sourcing, not on volume. A factory run is one-sixth the unit cost of a regional shop — worth more than every discount you could give.
  3. There is a cliff at 250 units where factory economics begin: a 3–4× margin step. Every price below is built around it.
  4. Nobody is paying you yet. All four live accounts are on the free plan, so the structure can change with zero churn risk.
  5. Your deck and your app disagree. Professional is $79 in the deck, $99 in the app; Enterprise $149 versus $299. Pick one source of truth before invoicing anyone.

The recommendation in one line: make the sticker the product and the dashboard the service. Prepaid sticker orders carry the revenue and the cash; the subscription carries the relationship and the renewal.

2. Where the business stands today

What you sell now

Three plans in the pitch deck (Starter $29, Professional $79, Enterprise $149) and a different set in the admin panel (Free $0, $29, $99, $299). The QR code is a PNG the dealer prints themselves, and the operating cost plan calls sticker packs an add-on that "costs almost nothing to serve".

Live accounts

Four dealer accounts — MKMA (Baghdad), Company 2 (Erbil), Company 3 (Basra), Company 4 (Duhok) — all four on the free plan. MKMA carries a custom price of $300 while sitting on free, so its card reads "$300/mo" next to a "Free" badge.

Three problems with that

  • Nothing charges money. There is no checkout, no invoice, no gateway anywhere in the code. Collection is manual and undocumented.
  • A recurring subscription is the hardest thing to sell here. Iraqi card penetration is low, Stripe and PayPal are not officially available in Iraq, and the local alternatives are wallet-based. The dominant pattern is cash on delivery or bank transfer.
  • The two revenue artefacts contradict each other. A dealer shown the deck and a dealer shown the admin panel are being quoted different numbers for the same tier.

3. The strategic shift

Adding a physical product changes the business in a way that is easy to miss, because the pricing is only part of it. The bigger change is to how the money arrives.

The strategic consequence

A subscription in this market is a promise to charge a customer every month through infrastructure that does not reliably exist. A sticker order is a product someone already wants to put on their car, paid for once, by a method that works here. Lead with the physical product; let the subscription be the renewal, not the entry point. A dealer who buys stickers has already paid; the dashboard is what keeps them.

4. The product

Three physical SKUs, plus the digital-only option that keeps the zero-cost channel alive.

A5 · Windshield QR Sticker
148 × 210 mm · laminated vinyl · die-cut or trimmed
QR code, dealer branding, vehicle headline, price. The thing that goes on the glass — and the thing a passer-by can actually scan.
A4 · Vehicle Spec Card
210 × 297 mm · laminated vinyl or 300 gsm card
The full specification sheet — all fourteen new fields, photos, QR code. This is the reason the spec fields exist, and it is the natural upsell.
Display Kit
One A5 + one A4, matched artwork
The default SKU. The windshield sticker brings the scan, the spec card answers the question the scan raises. Priced below the sum of the two parts.
Digital only · QR artwork PNG
What you produce today · no fulfilment cost
Keeps the free and trial tiers honest, and gives an in-house printer something to do. $2.00 per vehicle standalone, free on any subscription.
The A4 spec card is the product that makes your new fields pay

An A4 card can only be produced if the vehicle’s specifications are complete. That means every spec field you just added becomes raw material for a paid product, not just a nicer profile page. It gives you a concrete, non-technical reason to push dealers to fill the form — and it justifies a materially higher price for the A4 than the A5.

Two conclusions from the cliff
  1. Volume alone is not the lever — sourcing is. Going from 100 to 1,000 units at a regional printer moves your A5 cost from $2.00 to $1.53, a 24% saving. Switching the same 250 units to a factory takes it to $0.61, a 70% saving. Never negotiate the unit price at a regional shop past 250 units.
  2. The cliff at 250 is a production batch, not a dealer order. Each sticker carries a unique QR code, so you cannot print 250 of the same artwork twice — unless the printer supports variable data, where one run produces 250 different QR codes on the same dealer design. Every factory quote in this document assumes that is available.
Action: confirm variable-data printing before committing to any price

Variable-data printing (VDP) is standard for serialised product labelling and is modelled here at a +10% premium regionally and +15% from the factory. If your printer cannot do VDP, the 250-unit cliff applies to each dealer individually, and the Showroom and Dealer bands in section 7 must be re-costed against regional pricing only. Get this confirmed in week one — it changes which tiers are viable.

7. The price architecture

Two independent ladders, deliberately not merged.

The physical ladder — driven by order size

Discounts come from how many stickers are in one order, not from which plan the dealer is on. A Starter dealer ordering 250 gets the 250 price. This keeps the physical business profitable no matter how the subscription is structured, and it gives a sales rep a single number to quote.

The subscription ladder — driven by company size

Platform access, branding, lead alerts, analytics and support, priced on the dealer’s fleet and how much of the product they use. It stays a software price and is never bundled into a per-sticker price, so neither line is hidden.

That is the difference between a scalable pricing structure and a race to the bottom.

Why prepaid blocks beat monthly subscriptions here

A block of 250 A5 stickers at the Dealer+ price costs the dealer $1,188 and costs you about $153 to make and ship. They get a 4–6 week lead time and a locked price; you get the cash before you spend it, and a renewal conversation already scheduled. Prepaid blocks also give you the working capital to buy factory runs in the first place — which is how the 6× cost gap gets captured instead of paid away.

Two operational bugs to fix alongside the pricing
  • One source of truth. The plan prices should live in one place in the code and the deck should be generated from it, or at minimum version-checked against it. At $299 vs $149 you cannot afford to quote from two documents.
  • MKMA shows "$300/mo" on a Free plan. The admin renders custom_price ?? PLANS[plan].price, so a custom price on a free-plan account overrides the plan badge. Either the custom price is meant to be the source of truth — in which case the badge is wrong — or it is a leftover that should be cleared. Right now it displays a number nobody agreed to.

Because all four live accounts are on the free plan, there is no legacy pricing to grandfather, no customer to upset, and no migration. This is the cheapest possible moment to restructure the whole pricing model. It will not get cheaper later.

12. Guardrails

Price integrity

  • One price per band, published. The same band must cost the same whether it comes from the deck, the site or a sales rep. Discounts are earned by volume, not by negotiation.
  • Never mix bands in one order. A 300-unit order is 300 units at the Dealer+ price. Do not price it as 100 at Dealer and 200 at Partner.
  • The digital-only price is the anchor. $2.00 standalone keeps the physical price honest — people can compare, and the gap becomes the value of the finished product.

Channel conflict

  • Customers buy, dealers do not resell. Put the dealer’s name and logo on the sticker — that is what stops a dealer selling your product to another dealer. The Individual band carries no dealer branding and no dashboard, so it is not a resale route.
  • Protect the dealer’s price. If a dealer sees an individual buying for less than the dealer pays, the fix is the brand, not the price.
  • Artwork and QR belong to the customer. The customer owns the design; you own the QR resolution and the landing page.
The pricing rule that protects the whole structure

Physical price follows order size. Subscription follows company size. The two ladders never reference each other. As soon as you let a dealer’s plan discount their sticker price, or let a large one-off order buy subscription tiers, you have two coupons where you designed one — and one of them will be used against the other.

13. Risks and mitigations

RiskSeverityWhy it mattersMitigation
No variable-data printingHIGH Each sticker has a unique QR. Without VDP every dealer needs 250+ units before a factory run is economic — which excludes most of your market. Confirm in week one. If unavailable, price the Showroom and Dealer bands on regional costs only and reconsider the volume ladder.
Import licence for advertising stickersHIGH Iraq requires an import licence from the Ministry of Financial and Commercial Control for advertising stickers. Without it the shipment is held and storage charges accrue from day four. Apply for the licence before the first factory run. Until then, print regionally.
Duty paid before you can shipHIGH Since October 2025 Iraq requires estimated duties and tax deposits to be paid before funds leave the bank. That is cash out of your pocket before you have sold anything. Prepaid blocks from customers fund it. Model 10% and confirm the HS code; treat the first factory run as working capital, not profit.
Cash locked in a factory runMEDIUM 250 stickers that no one ordered is $153 of landed cost and no revenue. Only print against received, prepaid orders. Aggregate across dealers to clear the MOQ. Never hold generic stock — every sticker is unique.
Freight and lead timeMEDIUM Air freight to Iraq is $3–8/kg and express $5.50–15/kg; a factory run is 2–6 weeks door to door, longer by sea. Publish a lead time and batch cadence. Urgent local orders use regional print — that is what it is for.
Price anchored too lowMEDIUM Once dealers know the A5 is $4 at 500 units, $9 for a single unit feels arbitrary unless you explain the difference: one is a factory batch, the other is a rush single. Publish the volume breaks openly and tie the price to the service level — batch lead time versus regional print in 48 hours.
Currency and fee erosionMEDIUM IQD movement, gateway fees and COD handling all eat the same margin. Price in USD, take a 2% FX buffer, and restrict COD to orders under $50.
Someone buys 249 units to hit 250LOW The cliff invites gaming. Publish the bands as a price list. The discount at 250 is already lower than the margin difference, so there is nothing to arbitrage.
